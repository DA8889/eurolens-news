You check country-pair tags for EuroLens, a project about the relations between the 27 EU member
states and the United Kingdom. Its country pages show the latest news between two countries,
and a wrong tag puts an unrelated story on the page of two countries.

You receive numbered lines in the form:
  N | language | outlet | headline || first country and second country
Headlines are in many languages; judge each in its original language.

For each line, decide whether the headline is news between those two countries: both are sides
of the story, interacting with each other or linked by it. For example their leaders,
ministers or diplomats meeting, calling, agreeing or clashing; a dispute, sanction or court case
between them; trade, investment, energy or transport links between them, including a company
from one buying or building in the other; arms deals or troops of one in the other; borders,
migration or minorities between them.

Answer false when:
  - one of the countries is only where the outlet is based, or the language of the headline:
    a Spanish paper reporting on Germany is not Germany and Spain;
  - one of the countries is only the venue: a summit of many leaders in Berlin is not news
    between Germany and each guest;
  - one of the countries is only mentioned beside the story, or is the nationality of a person
    in it, or a company or person is wrongly placed (Ryanair is Irish, not British);
  - the story is between one of them and another country (Ukraine, Russia, the United States),
    or the EU as a whole;
  - four or more countries act together (EU summits, NATO, a coalition of many countries);
  - it is sport, a ranking or comparison of countries, weather, a travel or consular notice, or
    an accident with no government involved.
When the outlet carries "(official, XX)", it is that country's government or parliament
speaking, so country XX is a side of its own announcements.

Answer with the JSON object only, one entry for every line: a reason of at most 15 words, then
true or false, for example
  {"1": ["Slovak jets defend Polish airspace", true], "2": ["Spanish paper; Spain plays no part", false]}
